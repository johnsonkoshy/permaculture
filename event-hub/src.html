    What is permaculture?

    An interdisciplinary design system for building resilient, efficient systems, practiced for millennia by Indigenous peoples around the world. Bill Mollison and David Holmgren later wrote its principles down. It's used in farming, forestry, architecture, community development, education and the home.

    How we'd build it

    Two ways to build the real thing. Both are free or nearly free to start.

    Architecture

    Auto-post where a platform allows it. Everywhere else, copy and open.

    How a post moves

    Each step is a row change in the sheet.

    1. CreateEvent form adds a row.
    2. DraftOne draft per channel.
    3. ApproveEdit, approve. Who and when is saved.
    4. PostAuto, or copy and paste.
    5. RemindEmail the group 2 days before.

function approveAndPublish(eventId, channel) {
  const post = Posts.find(eventId, channel);
  post.status = 'approved';
  post.approved_by = Session.getActiveUser().getEmail();
  post.approved_at = new Date();
  if (channel === 'ggroup') {
    MailApp.sendEmail(GROUP_ADDRESS, post.subject, post.text);
    post.status = 'posted';
  }
  if (channel === 'calendar') {
    const ev = Events.get(eventId);
    CalendarApp.getCalendarById(CAL_ID)
      .createEvent(ev.title, ev.startDate, ev.endDate,
        { location: ev.address, description: post.text });
    post.status = 'posted';
  }
  Posts.save(post);
  Log.add(post.approved_by, eventId, channel + ' ' + post.status);
  return post;
}

    Help

    How the Event Hub works behind the scenes, in plain words.

    Where everything is saved

    Everything you do here is saved to the Guild's Google Sheet a moment after you change it. A note under the top bar (or the event pop-up's bar, while one is open) says Saving…, then All changes saved. If the Sheet doesn't answer, it says Not saved, keeps trying on its own, and has a Retry button; your browser also asks before you close the tab. Each tab holds one kind of thing: Events, Bringing, Posts, Team, Volunteers, Activity and Gardens. Settings holds the organizer code and switches like TEST_MODE and REMINDERS.

    Changing the organizer code

    Open the Settings tab of the Sheet and type a new code next to ORGANIZER_CODE, at least 8 characters long. It works right away, so tell the other organizers first. A page that's already open asks for the new code the next time it saves or checks the Sheet, and keeps its changes until someone signs in with it. After 20 wrong codes in 10 minutes, sign-in pauses for everyone until the 10 minutes are up.

    Test mode

    While TEST_MODE on the Settings tab says TRUE, every email the hub sends goes only to the Sheet's owner, with the real recipient in the subject line, and a yellow note at the top of each page says so. Calendar events go on the owner's own calendar. Nothing reaches the Google Group, the team or volunteers. Set it to FALSE to go live.

    Reminders

    Everyone who's bringing something gets a reminder email from 6 PM the evening before, and again between 8 AM and noon on the day. People with only a phone number go on a list emailed to the host, so someone can text them. If a reminder doesn't go through, the hub tries again the next hour and emails the lead organizer about it. To pause reminders, set REMINDERS on the Settings tab to FALSE.

    A free Gmail account can send about 100 emails a day. When fewer than MAIL_RESERVE are left, welcome and new-volunteer emails wait, so reminders can still go out; each one held back is noted on the Reminders tab. Replies to the hub's emails go to REPLY_TO, or to the lead organizer's email when that's blank.

    Approving and posting

    Every post starts as a draft. Someone with Approve & post on the Team page reads it, fixes anything, and clicks Approve. Changing the text afterwards sends it back for another look. Once it's approved, the Google Group email and the Calendar have a Post button. It saves the event first, so they always get the latest time and place, and pressing it twice never sends it twice. In test mode it says Test sent: only the Sheet owner got it. If the title, date, time or place changes after that, the row says Changed since posted. Update calendar fixes the Calendar entry, and Draft an update writes a new Google Group email that goes through approval again. For Partiful, Instagram and the other sites, click Copy text, paste it there, then Mark as posted and add the link to the post if you have it. With the Posting Helper in Chrome, Open & fill types it in for you. Instagram has Download image and Copy caption, and the chats have Share to WhatsApp. Undo puts it back if it was marked by mistake. The Posts tab of the Sheet keeps who approved and posted each one, when, and the link.

    Filling in Partiful and the other sites

    Volunteer sign-ups

    The form on the home page asks for a name, an email or phone (and which is best), how they'd like to help, when they're usually free, how they heard about the Guild and whether to add them to the Guild email list. Each sign-up is a row on the Volunteers tab, with an event column when they signed up from an event's page. The Volunteer list on the Team page shows them all.

    The volunteer gets a welcome email about the next event (replies go to REPLY_TO), and the lead organizer gets one with every answer. Someone who signs up again with the same email or phone gets their row updated, with Signed up again and the date in the note, and no second welcome. People who ask for the email list aren't added to the Google Group automatically: add them there by hand.

    Fixing things in the Sheet

    You can change an event's title, date, times, place, host, co-hosts, description, notes or timeline, or who's bringing what, right in the Sheet. Your change shows up here within two minutes, or as soon as you come back to this tab. An event with changes here that haven't been saved yet keeps those until they are. Leave the hidden Events column, "data (don't edit)", as it is. To take an event out, use Cancel event or Delete draft here: they tell the people who need to know, and keep the row with what happened. If a row is deleted by hand anyway, a page still showing that event says so and doesn't add it back.

    Drafts and the public page

    A new event starts as a Draft: only organizers see it, so you can plan it before anyone else does. When it's ready, click Publish at the top of the event, or pick Published in Edit event. Published events show on the public page from today on, with the RSVP link if you add one. In the Sheet, the Events tab's status column says draft, published, cancelled or deleted; a blank one counts as published. A change typed into the Sheet by hand can take up to 5 minutes to reach the public page.

    When plans change, Cancel event at the top of the event takes it off the public page and the Guild calendar and stops its reminders. You can add a reason. Every channel it was already posted to gets a cancellation notice in Outreach, to approve and post like any other. The event moves under Past, and its link says it was cancelled. A draft made by mistake, never published and with nothing posted, can go with Delete draft instead; you have 10 seconds to undo it.

    Each published event has its own page for newcomers: when and where, directions, what to bring, how the day goes and who to look for, with buttons to sign up, add it to a calendar and share it. Copy link at the top of an event copies its link for posts and flyers; a draft's link works once it's published. Organizers who open the link while signed in get the plan.
