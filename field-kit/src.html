Every neighbor who says hi gets a hello back, and a reason to return.

A field kit for the East Bay Permaculture Guild. It gives every flyer, post and text one shared look. It also adds simple tools that help a few volunteers welcome newcomers, learn how they want to take part, and turn one event into outreach for every channel.

The brief: welcoming and promoting falls on a handful of volunteers. Interest fades without follow-up. Keep it affordable, easy to maintain, and true to the personal connections that make the community worth joining.

What we found: the Guild's Linktree has more than 30 links, including Partiful, Hylo, Facebook, a Google Group, Instagram, Kinlia and Simbi. A newcomer has no obvious first step, and nobody owns the follow-up.

Steps 3 and 5 are where interest fades. Nobody follows up after the first hello, and nothing pulls people back after the first visit. Every tool below exists to cover one of those two gaps.

Brand system · v0.1

Sun, Sprout & Bay

Today the Guild has no shared look. Each Partiful invite, flyer and post is designed from scratch by whoever has time. This system gives the Guild one identity: a sage field, a cream line drawing of hills and water, a sprout, and a Meyer-lemon sun. It also sets typefaces that read well on a phone in full sun, and a voice that sounds like a neighbor.

Logo

A proposed mark: East Bay hills over the bay, a sprout, and the sun. It's simple enough to draw on a chalkboard and still reads as a 32px Partiful avatar.

Color

Sage is the ground, canopy and fern carry the type, and lemon is the sun. Use lemon on one thing per piece: the date, the button, the plant's name.

Rough proportions for any piece: 40 sage, 30 cream, 15 canopy, 10 fern, 5 lemon.

Type

Three faces, all free on Google Fonts. Young Serif is sturdy enough for a telephone pole and friendly enough for a thank-you card. Atkinson Hyperlegible was designed for low-vision readers, so it works for elders, kids and anyone reading in bright sun.

What we say

The three permaculture ethics, plus one principle that makes the first step easy. Every piece of outreach should land on at least one.

Ethic · Earth care

Regenerate the land

Observe and interact.

Name the plants, the creek, the pollinators. Be specific about what's coming back.

Ethic · People care

Neighbors, together

Integrate rather than segregate.

Lead with the people. Name the host. Say who'll be there and that newcomers are expected.

Ethic · Fair share

Share the surplus

Obtain a yield.

Potlucks, seed swaps, skill shares, extra figs. Something to take home and something to give.

Principle

Show up once

Use small and slow solutions.

Make the first step tiny: one Sunday morning, one plant, one reply.

How we sound

Like a neighbor

Write the way you'd talk over a fence. Use first names and say who's hosting.

Hi Maya, I'm Rosa. I live in Fruitvale too.
Dear Volunteer, thank you for registering.

Specific and practical

Give the date, the time, the place, what to bring and what you'll do. People decide on details.

Sun 11/15, 9:30. Planting garlic. Bring water.
Join us for an exciting regenerative event!

Open door

Assume they've never gardened. Say it's fine to come late, bring kids, or just watch.
